Cities of the Future

Talk about one feature of future cities

From prepared speech to spontaneous answers. Practise thinking, speaking and pronouncing clearly without memorising a script.

1. Listen and notice

Listen to the model. Repeat in short thought groups ( / ). Focus on the stressed syllables in bold.

One feature of future cities is smart public transporTAtion. / It will help reduce traffic conGEStion and air pollution. / For example, electric buses could carry more people / without producing exhaust fumes. / I think this will make cities cleaner and more LIVEable.

Pronunciation practice: Listen and repeat each word, then say it in a new sentence.

Listen to the prepared MP3 recordings. Audio files must be in the audio folder.

2. Build your own answer

Use four short parts: Feature → Description → Benefit → Example. Write or think of keywords only, not a full script.

Try speaking for 45 seconds about your chosen feature. Do not restart every time you make a mistake.

3. The surprise challenge

When you are ready, reveal a new follow-up question. You have 10 seconds to think and 30 seconds to speak. Use your own words.

Teacher's notes

Learning problem: Students can pronounce prepared speeches well but hesitate and make pronunciation errors when answering spontaneously.

Applied linguistics: During spontaneous speech, learners must plan ideas, retrieve vocabulary and articulate sounds simultaneously. Limited automaticity and cognitive processing demands may contribute to hesitation and pronunciation errors. Anxiety may also play a role.

Teaching approach: Model pronunciation, provide keyword-level scaffolding, gradually remove support, and use repeated spontaneous speaking tasks. Ask students to present one feature for 45 seconds, then answer an unseen follow-up for 30 seconds. Compare the first and second attempts for long pauses, intelligibility and ability to extend ideas. Avoid treating accent differences as errors or assuming that every learner has the same underlying difficulty.

This is a practice tool, not an automated pronunciation assessment.
